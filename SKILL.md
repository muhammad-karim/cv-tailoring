# CV tailoring

Source of truth: the candidate's master CV. Every fact, title, date and number comes from it. Nothing is invented, stretched or implied.

Precedence: the candidate's own drafting rules and career coach's advice come first. This skill adds to them. Where they disagree with this skill, theirs win.

## 1. Before writing anything

1. Read the full advert, plus any person spec or job pack (open linked PDFs).
2. Build a scorecard: list essential and desirable criteria in the employer's own words and order. With no person spec, take the 4 to 6 things the hiring manager will check (repeated words, anything in the first third of the advert).
3. Build an evidence map: for each criterion, pick the best evidence from the master CV, its number, and where it will sit on the CV.
4. Where there is no honest evidence, record it under "Gaps to check". Show adjacent experience only if described accurately.
5. If the advert cannot be read and no person spec exists, do not draft. Say what job pack is needed.

## 2. Format

- Start from the candidate's standard CV template so fonts, sizes, margins and header carry over.
- Name and position centred at the top. Contact links shown as the word only, hyperlinked. Email hyperlinked.
- Headings only: Areas of Expertise, Key Achievements, Professional Experience, Education.
- A4, two pages unless the template says otherwise. UK English. Single column. No text boxes, icons, skill bars or photo.
- Dates as "Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY", right-aligned, consistent.
- No date of birth, marital status, graduation years or references.
- Use the most accurate title for each role. Use a descriptive equivalent where the held title differs.
- File name: "<Name> - CV - <Organisation> - <Role>".

## 3. No profile statement on the CV

Do not add a profile statement to the CV. The template's four headings are the whole structure. Start straight into Areas of Expertise. Avoid the buzzword list anywhere on the page: passionate, results-driven, proven track record, dynamic, leverage, spearheaded, team player.

## 4. Bullets

Each bullet should carry four parts, in this order where it fits:
1. The skill term from the job advert, used in the employer's words, where it is true.
2. What was done, using a recognised sub-competency rather than a bare adjective.
3. Context and scale: budget, team size, markets, agencies, senior stakeholders.
4. The result, with a number where the master CV supports one.

Other rules:
- Mirror the advert's order. Lead page one with its top priorities.
- Lead with the result or scope when that is the headline. Otherwise use a specific verb.
- 3 to 6 bullets for recent roles, 0 to 2 for older roles. Older roles (roughly before 12 to 15 years) condense into "Earlier Career".
- Test every bullet: why does this recruiter need to know this? If not obvious, cut it.
- Marketing leadership roles: brand, growth, performance and commercial results first. Communications roles: PR, media relations, internal and change comms, stakeholders and speaking first.
- Moving between sectors: translate accurately (for example "supporters" to "customers", "income" to "revenue"). Never inflate.
- Use "led", "contributed to" and "part of a team" accurately. Never claim team results as the candidate's alone.
- For faith-based employers, lead with work that fits their values and leave out anything that conflicts with them.
- Self-employed or side ventures can stay out of a tailored CV, as long as nothing written implies otherwise.

## 5. Page one

- Title line: the advert's title only if the candidate has held it or a clear equivalent. Otherwise a truthful description.
- Areas of Expertise: 9 to 12 real skills, in the employer's language.
- Key Achievements: 5 to 6 bullets, each with organisation and number, in priority order. Do not repeat them word for word under roles.
- The most recent role starts on page one with its first 2 to 3 bullets.
- Top-third test: in about seven seconds, can a reader see title, sector fit and two numbers?

## 6. Keywords and honesty

- Use the employer's vocabulary only where it is true. List only skills the candidate really has, and no hidden keywords.
- Choosing what to include is fine. Creating a false impression is not. Test: would the employer feel misled if they knew the full truth behind this line?
- Never add or stretch a claim to raise a score.
- Do not rely on tables, text boxes or graphics: ATS parsing breaks on them.

## 7. Scoring (out of 10) and recording

Score the finished CV, not the fit. Round to the nearest 0.5 and do not round up.
- Essential criteria shown with specific evidence: 5 points. Multiply 5 by the share met, with half credit for adjacent evidence.
- Desirable criteria shown with specific evidence: 1 point. If none are listed, these points move to essential criteria.
- Mirroring (bullets follow the spec's order, top priorities lead page one, employer's words used): 2 points.
- Specificity and relevance (quantified bullets, scope shown, nothing unrelated): 2 points.
- Cap: if any essential criterion has no evidence, the score cannot exceed 7.
- With no person spec, use the scorecard's priorities as essential criteria and say so.

Save the scorecard, evidence map and score breakdown on the job's row in the candidate's job tracker, and the score in its CV score column.

## 8. Final checks before it goes out

- Export to PDF. Check the page count and that the PDF text extracts in the right order.
- Check every number and title against the master CV, and the employer's name throughout.
- Check links, spelling and formatting.
- Note for the candidate: rewrite the hook and close in your own words before sending.
